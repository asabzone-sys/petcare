Official Veterinary Nutrition Disclaimer

Feeding guidance and portion calculations provided by FurEver Care are intended strictly for general educational purposes. Individual metabolic rates, body condition scores (BCS), sterilisation status, and underlying medical conditions vary significantly. Always consult a licensed veterinarian before making drastic alterations to your pet's dietary regimen.

Canine Nutritional Roadmap

From weaning to golden senior years
Puppy (2 - 12 Months):

Higher protein (28%+) and DHA for brain and retinal development. 3 to 4 scheduled feedings daily to prevent hypoglycemia.

Adult Dog (1 - 7 Years):

Complete & balanced maintenance diet (22-26% protein, 12-16% fat). 2 structured meals daily to encourage regular digestion.

Senior Dog (7+ Years):

Moderate caloric density to curb weight gain, fortified with Glucosamine, Chondroitin, and L-carnitine for cardiac & joint mobility.

Feline Nutritional Roadmap

Obligate carnivore dietary requirements
Kitten (2 - 12 Months):

Energy-dense wet & dry formula loaded with essential Taurine, Arachidonic acid, and Calcium. Feed 3 to 4 portions daily.

Adult Cat (1 - 10 Years):

High moisture inclusion (mix wet food daily) to support renal and urinary tract health. Guard against indoor obesity.

Senior Cat (10+ Years):

Highly digestible protein with controlled phosphorus levels for kidney support and elevated omega fatty acids for cognitive vitality.

Food Safety Guide

Safe Treats vs. Dangerous Toxic Foods

Review what is healthy to share as a reward and what must be strictly avoided.

Pet-Safe Superfoods & Rewards

  • Pumpkin (Plain, canned): Superb soluble fiber for soothing acute diarrhea or constipation.
  • Blueberries: Low-calorie antioxidant powerhouse safe for dogs and cats.
  • Carrots (Raw or steamed): Great crunch for canine dental health with vitamin A.
  • Cooked Salmon / Whitefish: Lean protein rich in EPA & DHA (never raw).
  • Green Beans (Unsalted): High-volume, low-calorie treat for dogs on weight loss plans.

Strictly Toxic Substances (Never Feed)

  • Chocolate & Caffeine: Theobromine causes cardiac arrhythmia and seizures.
  • Xylitol (Birch Sugar): Found in sugar-free peanut butter; causes rapid liver failure.
  • Grapes & Raisins: Highly nephrotoxic; can trigger acute renal failure in dogs.
  • Onions, Garlic, Chives: Causes oxidative damage to feline & canine red blood cells.
  • Cooked Bones: Splinters easily, causing severe gastrointestinal punctures.
Veterinary Notice: The calculations and dietary suggestions provided here are guidelines. Always consult your personal veterinarian or veterinary nutritionist regarding specific medical diets, allergies, or therapeutic requirements.
