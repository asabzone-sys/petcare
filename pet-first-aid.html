Pet First Aid & Emergency Protocols

Immediate clinical guidelines to stabilize your dog or cat before reaching a veterinary hospital. Always transport your pet immediately after emergency stabilization.

Call 24/7 Hotline Emergency Hospitals
Step-by-Step CPR

Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (Dogs & Cats)

1

Check Responsiveness & Breathing

Gently touch the animal and call their name. Look for chest movement and check the pulse at the femoral artery (inner upper thigh).

2

Position on Right Side (Lateral Recumbency)

Lay pet on their right side on a firm, flat surface. Ensure head and neck are aligned in a straight path.

3

Chest Compressions (100–120 BPM)

Place hands over widest part of ribcage. Compress 1/3 to 1/2 chest width. Perform 30 rapid, rhythmic compressions.

4

Rescue Breaths (Mouth-to-Snout)

Hold pet's mouth firmly closed. Place mouth over their nose and blow 2 gentle breaths until you see the chest rise. Repeat 30:2 cycle.

Compression Rhythm Assistant

Maintain the recommended 110 beats-per-minute compression pace (matching the beat of the song 'Stayin' Alive').

Choking & Foreign Object Airway Clearance

1. Visual Mouth Inspection

Carefully open jaw using one hand on upper muzzle and one on lower jaw. Only sweep if object is clearly visible and loose to prevent pushing it deeper.

2. Gravity & Back Blows

For small dogs and cats, hold pet head-downwards. Deliver 4–5 sharp palm strikes between shoulder blades.

3. Pet Heimlich Maneuver

Stand behind dog, place fist under belly behind rib cage, and thrust firmly upward toward spine 4–5 times.

Emergency Household Toxin Index

Do NOT induce vomiting with hydrogen peroxide unless explicitly instructed by a veterinarian.

Chocolate & Caffeine

Theobromine triggers severe arrhythmia and seizures. Dark chocolate and cocoa powder carry highest toxicity.

Xylitol / Birch Sugar

Causes rapid insulin surge and profound hypoglycemia within 30 minutes, leading to acute liver failure.

Lilies (Extremely Fatal to Cats)

Even pollen ingestion or drinking vase water triggers irreversible feline renal failure within 36 hours.

Grapes & Raisins

Tartaric acid content triggers acute kidney injury in dogs regardless of quantity ingested.

Onions, Garlic & Leeks

Organosulfoxides cause oxidative hemolysis and hemolytic anemia in red blood cells.

Human Painkillers (NSAIDs & Paracetamol)

Acetaminophen destroys feline hemoglobin; Ibuprofen triggers stomach perforation and kidney collapse.

Hyperthermia & Heatstroke Cooling Protocol

Body temperature exceeding 104°F (40°C) is a critical emergency. DO NOT use ice-cold water or ice baths, as extreme cold causes peripheral vasoconstriction, trapping core heat inside internal organs.

1. Safe Active Evaporative Cooling

Apply cool or lukewarm tap water to paw pads, groin, and ears. Place a fan blowing gently over the damp fur to speed evaporative heat loss.

2. Stop Cooling at 103°F (39.4°C)

Cease active cooling once temperature drops to prevent rebound hypothermia. Transport in an air-conditioned vehicle immediately.

Arterial & Venous Hemorrhage Control

Direct Firm Pressure:

Press sterile gauze or clean cotton towel directly over the wound for at least 5 unbroken minutes without lifting.

Never Remove Soaked Gauze:

If blood seeps through, place fresh layers directly on top to preserve forming clot structures.

Elevation & Pressure Wrap:

Elevate wounded limb above the heart level if no fracture is suspected. Secure snug elastic self-adherent bandage.
